# What these pages are missing

*Measured 5 September 2026, answering the founder: are the contents the same
across the roles and the product, and what is essential to fill them.*

---

## 1. The measurement

Chrome stripped (nav, footer, chat, cookie bar), every text block over 18
characters compared across the five pages.

| Page | Blocks | Words | Shares with /product |
|---|---|---|---|
| /product | 51 | 1,128 | — |
| /teachers | 35 | 832 | **28 of 35 — 80%** |
| /schools | 34 | 831 | 12 of 34 — 35% |
| /students | 12 | 423 | 0 |
| /parents | 15 | 402 | 0 |

Role-to-role overlap: **/teachers ↔ /schools = 0 blocks. /students ↔
/parents = 1.**

## 2. What that actually says

**The roles do not repeat each other.** That instinct was wrong, and the data
is unambiguous — zero shared blocks between the two pages that matter.

**The duplication is role → product.** 40 of /product's 51 blocks are
reprints: 28 from /teachers, 12 from /schools. /product is not a page, it is
the other two concatenated. It has no argument of its own, which is why
reading it after a role page feels like re-reading.

**And everything is thin.** MagicSchool's equivalent pages run 2,700–5,700
words. Ours run 400–1,128 — a quarter to a seventh. When a page has this
little on it, the shared parts dominate whatever is unique, which is the
second half of why it all reads the same.

**So the fault is not four pages saying one thing.** It is one page (/product)
with nothing of its own, and five pages that are all too short to have a
character.

## 3. What /product should be instead

Not a catalogue of what each role touches — that is what the role pages are
for. /product should be the only page that explains **the system**: that these
modules are a loop, not a list, and that the assistant is what turns it.

That content already exists and is unused on the site — `docs/modules.md`:

- **The loop.** Plan → Teach → Prepare → Assess → See → back into planning
- **T4 Period Log as the spine** — five modules read from it, and one teacher
  saying what she covered is what makes the rest mean anything
- **The assistant as three things at once** — the interface, the capture
  device, the engine — which is why it is not a feature beside the others

That is a genuine product page and nothing on it belongs on a role page.

## 4. Essential content, per page

### Everywhere (missing on all five)

| What | Why it is essential | Who writes it |
|---|---|---|
| **Real product screenshots** | Every tile panel is a stand-in I composed. This is the single biggest gap — a school buying software wants to see it | Founder / product |
| **Real quotes** | All testimonials are placeholders. MagicSchool runs a "Wall of Love"; we run invented names | Founder — from pilot |
| **An FAQ block** | On every single MagicSchool page. Ours has one, on /pricing only | I can draft from the brief |
| **Data handling and security** | Their strongest page, our thinnest sentence, and the first thing a school asks. Who owns the data, where it lives, is it used for training, what happens when we leave | Founder — needs the real answer |
| **Onboarding: what actually happens in week one** | The unanswered objection on every page | Founder |

### /product — needs a spine of its own

- The loop, as a diagram and as an argument *(content exists in modules.md)*
- Where the copilot sits in it and why that makes it not-a-feature *(exists)*
- What the school has to supply: syllabus, rubric, register format, class lists
- What integrates with what — or honestly, that nothing does yet

### /teachers — needs specificity

- **How the rubric is set**, since every marking claim rests on it
- **What a teacher supplies on day one** — syllabus, subject books, class list
- **Accuracy and the review step**: what happens to handwriting it cannot read
- **A real teacher quote** about marking, not a generic one
- Subjects and levels it actually works for today

### /schools — needs buyer content, and has almost none

This page has to survive a purchase decision and currently answers none of it:

- **Rollout**: who does the setup, how long, what the office does vs. what we do
- **Migration**: existing student records, staff, past academics
- **Pricing posture** — /pricing exists but the buyer page never mentions cost
- **Security, compliance, data ownership**
- **Who else uses it** — even one pilot school named
- **What happens if it goes wrong**: support, and what breaks the day it is off
- A principal or office quote

### /students and /parents — decide, then fill or demote

400 words each. Either they get real content — what a student actually sees on
their record, an example of what a parent actually receives — or they become
sections of /product and leave the nav. My recommendation is still the second:
neither audience buys and neither searches.

## 5. The numbers, when a pilot exists

Slots to design now and fill later — never before:

1. Hours returned per teacher per week *(their claim: 7–10)*
2. Answer sheets marked, and **the share the teacher changed** — the trust
   number, and ours alone to claim
3. Parents reached on the day, vs. the group message
4. Papers set per teacher per term, before and after

## 6. Order I would do it in

1. **Give /product its own spine** — the loop. Fixes the 80% duplication and
   needs no new facts
2. **Fill /schools with buyer content** — it is the page that has to close, and
   it is the emptiest relative to its job
3. **Screenshots** — unblocks every page at once
4. **FAQ + data handling** as shared blocks, the way MagicSchool does
5. **Demote /students and /parents**
6. **Tool pages** *(see competitive-audit.md)* — new search demand, no new claims
