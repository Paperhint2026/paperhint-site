# Founding-school partnership — the procedure

How a school goes from first contact to running partner. Internal
playbook; the school-facing version is the pitch deck (/pitch) and the
"What happens next" steps on the contact page. Nothing here promises
what doesn't ship (no analytics, no digital exams).

---

## Phase 0 — Qualify (before anyone travels)

1. School reaches out (contact form / referral) or we shortlist them.
2. One call with the decision-maker (principal / correspondent):
   - How does correction work today? Paper setting? Attendance?
   - Sections × students × exams per term (sizing, and later pricing).
   - Who would champion this in the staff room? (Name a teacher.)
3. Ask them to bring to the demo: **one class's answer sheets from a
   real exam + that exam's question paper and marking scheme**, and a
   photo of their attendance register.
4. Internally: run their numbers through the cost model (private —
   docs/paperhint-cost-model.xlsx). ≥50% margin rule holds.

**Exit:** a demo date, a named champion teacher, real paper coming.

## Phase 1 — The demo (their school, not a sample)

1. Before the meeting: set up their school in the portal — real class
   names, one section, their subjects. Never demo on "Greenfield
   Public School" if we can avoid it.
2. In the room: photograph their answer sheets live. Marks come back
   on their marking scheme. The teacher reviews, adjusts, approves —
   they must touch the approve button themselves.
3. Draft one question paper for a chapter THEY name, from their
   syllabus. Scan their attendance register.
4. Do the savings arithmetic on their numbers (deck slide 9), live.
5. Leave behind: the one-page feature sheet (docs/paperhint-features.pdf).

**Exit:** the school asks about pricing — or tells us why not (record
the objection; it feeds the roadmap).

## Phase 2 — Pilot agreement

1. Scope: which classes/sections start (recommend: 2–3 sections, one
   department), which modules first (attendance is live and easiest;
   evaluation lands the punch).
2. Pricing: per enrolled student, set together in the founding window;
   pro-rated to their academic year. Founding rate stays theirs.
3. Data terms in writing: school's data scoped to school's account,
   never sold or shared; what happens to data if the pilot ends.
4. Name the two sides: their champion teacher + office point-person;
   our founder-in-charge for this school.

**Exit:** signed pilot terms, a start date inside two weeks.

## Phase 3 — School setup (office first)

1. Admin portal populated: classes, sections, students (roll numbers,
   parent contacts), teachers (school emails), subjects.
2. Subject books/materials attached — they flow to department
   teachers' libraries and tune drafted content.
3. Teacher-to-class allotments entered (or worked out with the
   allotment automation).
4. We do this WITH the office, hands-on, in one sitting if possible.
   Migration of existing records is on us, not homework for them.

**Exit:** every teacher logs in and sees their own classes.

## Phase 4 — Teachers on (one staff-room hour)

1. One session in the staff room, champion teacher co-hosting.
2. Each teacher leaves having done three things themselves:
   drafted a note with the copilot, set one homework (parents held
   until Phase 6), drafted one question paper for their next test.
3. Shared library explained: private by default, share = school-wide.
4. WhatsApp line open to us for the pilot period — questions answered
   by a founder, same day.

**Exit:** every pilot teacher has one artifact of their own in their
library.

## Phase 5 — The first live loop (the proof)

1. Pick the next scheduled exam in a pilot section — never a special
   "pilot exam"; the school's normal calendar.
2. Paper drafted in Paperhint (teacher edits and approves), printed
   and conducted on paper as always.
3. Answer sheets photographed same day; teacher reviews and approves
   marks; results land on student records.
4. Attendance scanning running daily in pilot sections through this.
5. We are on-site or on-call for the first evaluation day.

**Exit:** one full exam cycle — set, written, marked, recorded — with
teacher approval at every step. This is the moment the school decides.

## Phase 6 — Parents on

1. Notification triggers switched on for pilot sections: homework on
   assignment, absence same-day, marks when the teacher shares them.
2. Office sends a one-time note to parents (we draft it for them) so
   the first notification isn't a surprise.

**Exit:** first week of parent notifications with zero office effort.

## Phase 7 — Custom modules (the partnership dividend)

1. Standing rule: what the staff asks for lands on the roadmap; the
   founding school's way of working shapes what every school gets.
2. Custom asks triaged monthly with the champion + office:
   - config, not code (do it now)
   - a module worth building (roadmap, with their name on it)
   - out of scope (say so plainly — no promise-and-forget)
3. Ecosystem work: bring the applications they already run into one
   place where feasible; scoped case by case.

**Exit criteria: none — this loop is the partnership.**

## Phase 8 — Review, rollover, expansion

1. End-of-term review with the principal: the savings arithmetic
   re-run on actuals (their marking hours, their paper evenings).
2. Expansion: more sections/departments at the same founding rate.
3. Year end: batch migration — promote classes in one move, archive
   the year, subjects/books carry forward.
4. Ask for the testimonial and the referral only after Phase 5 has
   run at least twice.

---

## The two rules that govern all of it
- **Their paper, their approval.** Nothing is marked, sent, or filed
  without a teacher or the office signing off.
- **Never promise past the brief.** Analytics, dashboards, digital
  exams — not until they ship.
