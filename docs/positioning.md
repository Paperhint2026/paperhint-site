# What Paperhint is

*My understanding of the founder's brief of 3 September 2026, written down so
the writing has a spine. **This is not page copy** — it is the argument the
copy has to make. Where it and a page disagree, one of them is wrong; check
with the founder which.*

---

## The one line

**Paperhint makes a school faster.**

Not by moving it online. Not by taking paper away. By putting an assistant on
every desk that prepares, remembers and follows through — so the hours go to
teaching instead of to the work stacked around it.

## What we sell

**Capability, and speed.**

A teacher who walks in prepared, every class, every section. A class that is
ready before it starts. A school that can see itself and plan from what it
sees. Work at a scale and a pace that hand-work never reached, and work a
school simply could not do at all before.

Describe what the person can now **do**. Not what they do less of, and not the
chores that disappear — a run of artefacts is a description of the old world
with a tick beside each line. Say what the new day looks like.

Three tests for a sentence before it ships:

1. Does it name something the reader can now do, or somewhere they get to
   faster? If it names a task that has gone away, rewrite it.
2. Would it still be worth saying to a school that had no problem with its
   current way of working? If not, it is leaning on their pain rather than
   your value.
3. Is the subject the teacher or the school — not the software?

## The teacher's day — what is already done when they get there

A teacher walks into a class. Across that day and that week they must:

- take attendance, and get it recorded
- set homework, and make sure home hears about it
- prepare notes for the next class, and the session after that
- look something up — a portion they want to teach better, or check
- mark answer sheets and assignments after every test, for every section
- keep the marks, per student, in a form that means something later
- borrow a period from another teacher, or cover one
- follow a timetable and be where they said they would be

Every one of those is a job today. Paperhint is the assistant that does them
alongside the teacher: it reminds, it prepares, it drafts, it evaluates, it
carries the follow-through. It is also the knowledge source at the desk — so a
teacher stops hunting across the internet, gathering, printing and stacking it
by the register.

**The promise: teach better, because everything around the teaching is already
done when you get there.**

## The school's day — what the office can now run

- setting up a class, and migrating the whole school year on year
- curating sources and building the knowledge every teacher draws on
- managing teachers; managing students; managing classrooms
- notifications home — today a teacher types a WhatsApp message into a group
  to tell parents about an assignment, a test, a homework
- every class logged, every mark sheet monitored
- the numbers: school performance, scoring across sections, at a scale that
  today means typing into a spreadsheet and mailing it up the line, for a
  budget conversation and a planning conversation

None of that scales by hand. Paperhint is where it is configured once and then
runs — and where the picture of the school lives, so the people running it can
plan and strategise from something real.

## Where it is going — a digital school

Today there are two roles: **teachers** and **the office**. That is the
beginning, not the shape.

The direction is a school that runs digitally end to end, every role
intertwined in one system, each with its own road toward it:

- **A committee role** — for independent schools especially: curating the
  syllabus structure, deciding what is taught and how, defining rubrics and
  method, planning the year.
- **A student role** — not a portal to check marks, but something that helps a
  student study better: their classroom notes, their logs, an assistant that
  teaches.
- **Parents** — today they receive; there is more of that relationship to build.

Say this as *direction*, never as availability. See the rule below.

## AI, and how we say it

AI runs through the platform and does almost all of this work. That is the
truth and we should not be coy about it.

But it is still not the headline, and it is never the noun. We do not sell "an
AI for schools". We sell what a teacher gets — notes prepared, sheets marked,
parents told, the week carried — and AI is how. Name it in the detail, in the
mechanics, in the answer to "how does that work". Never on the face of a page
as the product itself.

## Only say what ships

`docs/modules.md` is the module map with the founder's own status tags. The
website may describe **DONE** and **TWEAK** modules as things a school gets.
Anything tagged **BUILD** is direction, written as direction, with no date.

This is not theoretical: on 3 September the site claimed a school could see
every class logged and every mark sheet monitored and plan a budget from it
(A14, not started), start mid-term with timetables (A3, not started), and
borrow a colleague's period by asking (T2, not started) — and a placeholder
testimonial had a vice principal praising the timetable module. All corrected.
Check any new claim against the map before it ships.

Live today, in short: the assistant on both sides · knowledge sources ·
personal and shared libraries · teaching notes · homework · question papers ·
grading · results · attendance and its oversight · classes and sections ·
subjects and books · students · teachers · allotments.


**What "ships" means, as of 5 September 2026 (founder):** every module in the
module map's BUILD column is live by the time the first school is onboarded,
so the site may show it as the product — the timetable included. The line is
**Phase 2** (Classwork / Quick Quiz, PTM One-pager, Remedial Follow-up): those
stay off the site until they exist. Check any new claim against
`docs/modules.md` before it goes on a page.

## The rules that do not move

1. **Never promise what has not shipped.** The committee role and the student
   role are the vision, and must be written as vision — "where this is going",
   not "what you get". No dates, ever. If someone asks whether it is available:
   it is not, today there are two roles.
2. **Exams stay on pen and paper.** This is not a digital examination portal.
   Still true, still worth saying — as reassurance, not as the pitch.
3. **Never invent** a price, a number, a percentage, a customer.
4. **Words that do not appear**, in any tense or paraphrase: digitise,
   streamline, leverage, solutions, empower, seamless, optimise, robust — and
   any phrasing that measures the product by what a school does less of.

## The shape of a page, under this positioning

1. What the person can now do, and how much faster.
2. The day or the week it changes, concretely.
3. The mechanics — where AI does the work, named plainly.
4. The reassurance: exams stay on paper, nothing to migrate, one licence.
5. Where it is going, if the page warrants it.
